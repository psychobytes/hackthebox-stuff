# [Hatsune Miku no Shoushitsu] When Hatsune Miku “Disappeared” from the Internet

---
date: "August 25, 2026"
category : "Articles"
Tags : "Hatsune Miku"
---

#### In October 2007, the Japanese internet witnessed a strange event. Hatsune Miku was in the middle of an explosion of popularity, yet when people tried to search for her on Google and Yahoo!, she appeared to have vanished.

## A Phenomenon That Made No Sense

Hatsune Miku was released on August 31, 2007. Within just a few weeks, the VOCALOID software and its turquoise-haired virtual singer had become a rapidly growing phenomenon across the Japanese internet.

Songs, illustrations, videos, and other fan-made works were appearing everywhere.

Then, on October 17, 2007, something strange happened.

Searching for **「初音ミク」 (Hatsune Miku)** through image search engines no longer produced the results people expected.

On Yahoo! Japan, the search reportedly returned **zero results**. Google Image Search also experienced problems, displaying images unrelated to Hatsune Miku. Meanwhile, other search engines, including Microsoft's Live Search, goo, and livedoor, were still able to find images of her.

The incident quickly became a major topic of discussion across the Japanese internet.

---

## Did Miku Become Too Popular?

Over the years, the incident has developed its own popular explanation within the Vocaloid community.

One Reddit user summarized the story as follows:

> “Back in 2007, a few months after Miku was released, her explosive popularity caused a bug where the Google engine thought an attack was occurring due to the sheer flood of searches so her name was blocked.”

This explanation is easy to understand: **Hatsune Miku became popular so quickly that the search engine supposedly interpreted the sudden flood of searches as suspicious activity.**

However, there is an important distinction to make.

This explanation is a **community interpretation of the incident**, not an official explanation provided by Google or Yahoo!.

Still, it perfectly captures why the event has become such an enduring piece of Hatsune Miku lore.

For a brief moment, Miku had seemingly become **too popular for the internet to handle**.

---

## The Internet Begins to Speculate

Because Hatsune Miku suddenly became difficult to find through some of the largest image search engines at the time, speculation quickly spread.

Was the keyword **「初音ミク」** being filtered?

Had Hatsune Miku-related content been classified as spam?

Had someone requested that the search results be removed?

Or was something else happening entirely?

The incident became a major topic on Japanese internet forums, particularly **2channel (2ch)**. Discussions rapidly multiplied, with users proposing everything from technical failures to deliberate censorship.

The situation became even stranger because, around the same time, the Hatsune Miku article on Japanese Wikipedia was also temporarily deleted.

The Wikipedia deletion was eventually explained as a normal editorial issue involving content copied from the official website without proper attribution. However, because it happened around the same time as the image search incident, many users connected the two events and further fueled speculation.

For a short period, it genuinely looked as if Hatsune Miku was disappearing from the internet.

---

## Google and Yahoo! Investigate

On October 18, Japanese technology news outlets began contacting the search engine companies directly.

Google acknowledged that there was a problem and stated that it was investigating the issue. Yahoo! Japan also confirmed that searches for **「初音ミク」** were returning abnormal results and said it was looking into the cause.

Importantly, there was no evidence that Crypton Future Media—the company behind Hatsune Miku—had requested the removal of the search results.

Both the search engine companies and Crypton denied that Miku had been intentionally removed.

A few days later, on October 23, Hatsune Miku images began returning to Google Image Search and Yahoo! Japan, although some unrelated results reportedly remained.

Miku had returned.

---

## So, What Actually Happened?

On October 26, Yahoo! Japan finally provided a clearer explanation.

According to Yahoo!, the problem was related to delays in collecting and indexing image data for a keyword that had suddenly become extremely popular.

In other words, the system had difficulty keeping up with the explosive growth of searches and content surrounding Hatsune Miku.

Yahoo! explicitly denied that the keyword had been intentionally removed or classified as spam.

This means that the popular explanation—

> *“Google thought the flood of Hatsune Miku searches was an attack.”*

—should be treated as an interpretation that emerged from the community rather than a confirmed technical explanation.

What can be confirmed is simpler:

* Hatsune Miku suddenly became difficult to find through Google and Yahoo! image searches.
* Yahoo! Japan temporarily returned zero results for her name.
* Google Image Search displayed unrelated images.
* Other search engines were still able to find Hatsune Miku normally.
* Google, Yahoo!, and Crypton denied that the removal was intentional.
* Yahoo!'s eventual explanation pointed to delays in collecting and indexing image data for an unexpectedly popular keyword.

---

## The Disappeared Hatsune Miku

The incident later became known as **「消えた初音ミク」 (*Kieta Hatsune Miku*)**, or **“The Disappeared Hatsune Miku.”**

The name is wonderfully ironic.

At the time, Hatsune Miku was not actually disappearing from the internet.

The opposite was happening.

The internet was becoming flooded with her.

Thousands of illustrations, songs, videos, and fan creations were appearing at an incredible pace. Yet, for several days, some of the largest search engines on the internet struggled to find her.

Hatsune Miku had not disappeared.

**For a brief moment, the systems designed to find things on the internet simply could not keep up with her.**

---

## A Song About Miku's Disappearance

There is another connection that makes the story of Miku's disappearance
even more interesting.

In November 2007, only a few weeks after the search engine incident,
cosMo@BousouP released a song titled **「初音ミクの消失 -DEAD END-」**,
officially known in English as **THE END OF HATSUNE MIKU -DEAD END-**.

The song tells a fictional story in which Hatsune Miku realizes that she
is only a digital imitation of a human. She knows that she can be deleted,
yet she still wants to continue singing.

Eventually, Miku is uninstalled.

The song was released on November 8, 2007, while the longer version,
**THE END OF HATSUNE MIKU (LONG VERSION)**, followed in April 2008.
cosMo@BousouP later released a 2018 remake for Miku's 10th anniversary. 
:contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

![THE END OF HATSUNE MIKU by cosMo@BousouP](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/6650614b-b738-4462-ac72-6897a38b16b7)

*THE END OF HATSUNE MIKU — a song about the disappearance and eventual
deletion of a digital Miku.*

It is important to clarify that **THE END OF HATSUNE MIKU was not written
as an official retelling of the October 2007 Google/Yahoo! incident**.
The song has its own fictional story about Miku being deleted.

However, the timing is striking.

In October 2007, people briefly thought Hatsune Miku had disappeared from
the internet.

One month later, a song about Hatsune Miku herself disappearing was
released.

Whether intentional or simply coincidence, the two events have become
closely associated in the mythology surrounding early Hatsune Miku.

The real incident was about a search engine failing to find her.

The song was about Miku being deleted.

Both, in their own way, asked the same strange question:

**What happens when Hatsune Miku disappears?**
